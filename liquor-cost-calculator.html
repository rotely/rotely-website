How liquor cost works

Liquor cost is what the liquor you poured cost you, divided by what you sold it for. You need two counts, your invoices in between, and your liquor sales for the same days.

Say your last count was $4,200 of liquor at cost. You bought $2,600 more. Today's count is $3,900. So you went through $4,200 + $2,600 − $3,900 = $2,900 of liquor. If your liquor sales for those days were $14,500, your liquor cost is $2,900 ÷ $14,500 = 20%. Before taxes, you kept 80 cents of every dollar.

Why it's higher than you expect

  • Heavy pours. A 2 ounce pour on a 1.5 ounce recipe is a third more liquor in every drink.
  • Comps, spills and staff drinks that never ring up.
  • A price went up on an invoice and the drink price didn't.
  • An invoice that never got added, or a count on the wrong day, so the two dates don't match your sales.

Get a number you can trust

Count on the same day of the week, before you open, and use the sales from the same days the counts cover. Value the shelf at what you paid, not what it sells for. Check it after every full count. One bad week is noise. Three in a row means something changed.
